How a brokered petroleum deal works

In a brokered deal, the broker never takes ownership of the product. Our job is to find a counterparty who can genuinely perform, verify them, and introduce the buyer and seller under signed confidentiality and non-circumvention terms.

From there the principals negotiate directly and sign a Sales & Purchase Agreement. The broker stays involved to coordinate documents, inspection and timelines, and is paid an agreed fee, usually per delivered lift.

Guide

FOB, CIF and CFR in plain terms

  • FOB (Free On Board): the seller loads the cargo at the origin port. From then on the buyer arranges and pays for freight and insurance.
  • CFR (Cost and Freight): the seller pays freight to the destination port, and the buyer insures the cargo.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight): the seller pays both freight and insurance to the destination port.

The right term depends on who controls shipping best. Always reference the Incoterms® 2020 rules in the contract.

Due diligence

Red flags in fuel offers

Fuel trading attracts fraud. Be cautious of any offer that:

  • is priced far below the market benchmark,
  • asks for an upfront payment before proof of product,
  • pushes you to sign within hours,
  • names a refinery or port that doesn't match the product grade,
  • uses procedures that a trade-finance bank would not recognise.

A genuine counterparty welcomes verification. We check before we introduce.
